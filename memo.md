# Which lever moves LTV:CAC most?

**Summary.** In the base case, a hypothetical Indian SMB payroll SaaS earns ₹1.73 of lifetime gross profit per ₹1 of acquisition cost and needs 20.2 months to recover it. Improving each lever by 20%: price and demo-to-paid conversion tie (+20%), then churn (+16%), top-of-funnel conversion (+12%) and seat expansion (+5%). Work on demo-to-paid conversion first. All inputs are assumptions or public benchmarks, not company data.

## Base case

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Blended ARPA | ₹4,086 per month |
| Blended CAC | ₹61,919 |
| LTV (with expansion, 60-month cap) | ₹106,858 |
| LTV:CAC | 1.73 |
| CAC payback | 20.2 months |
| 12-month net revenue retention | 78% |

Payback is near the ~23-month private SaaS median (KeyBanc and Sapphire Ventures, 2022).

## Each lever improved by 20%

| Lever | LTV:CAC | Change | Payback (months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price up | 2.07 | +20.0% | 16.8 |
| Demo held-to-paid up | 2.07 | +20.0% | 16.8 |
| Monthly churn down | 2.01 | +16.2% | 20.2 |
| Visitor-to-sign-up up | 1.94 | +12.2% | 18.0 |
| Seat expansion up | 1.81 | +4.8% | 20.2 |

## Recommendation

Start with demo-to-paid conversion. It matches a price rise without the risk of losing deals: a 20% price rise gains nothing if it cuts that conversion by 16.7%.

Churn is next, and leads when retention is weak: at 5% monthly churn (bear case) it ranks first (+21%). Top-of-funnel gains are smaller because extra sign-ups also add sales cost. Seat expansion barely matters: 20% of a 1% monthly rate is 0.2 points, against 0.6 for churn.

Two findings matter more than the ranking:

- At 3% churn, reaching the common 3x rule of thumb needs demo held-to-paid of about 24%, against 14% today.
- At one blended CAC, 10–25 employee accounts return 0.73x and 101–500 employee accounts 6.3x. Segment choice may beat any single lever.

## Limits

- Benchmarks are generic and mostly from US agency client data, not Indian HR SaaS.
- Levers act alone: price does not affect conversion or churn, and churn and CAC are equal across segments.
- CAC is steady-state: no sales ramp, capacity limits, onboarding cost or GST.
- LTV is capped at 60 months. A longer cap makes churn look more important.

## Three questions before trusting this model

1. What are 12-month logo and revenue churn by headcount segment, and how much comes from companies shutting down rather than switching vendor? Only switching is churn the team can prevent.
2. What does it cost, including implementation, to win a 15-employee account versus a 200-employee one? If similar, small accounts lose money at today's prices.
3. When price or discounts last changed, what happened to demo-to-paid conversion? This tests the weakest assumption: that price can rise without losing deals.
